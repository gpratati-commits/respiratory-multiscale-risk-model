# Multi-Scale Forecasting of Respiratory Virus-Associated Disease Burden

## Project aim

To investigate whether epidemiological surveillance, cross-pathogen activity,
environmental conditions, and population contact patterns can improve short-term
prediction and understanding of respiratory infectious disease burden.

A later extension will investigate whether airway microbiome characteristics
can improve prediction of respiratory exacerbations in patients with chronic
airway disease.

## Primary public-data research question

Can recent activity of influenza, RSV and SARS-CoV-2, together with seasonality
and environmental predictors, improve short-term prediction of respiratory
disease burden compared with models based only on recent outcome history?

## Initial study setting

Singapore.

## Forecast horizons

- 1 week ahead
- 2 weeks ahead
- 4 weeks ahead

## Planned models

- Persistence baseline
- Moving-average baseline
- Seasonal naive baseline
- Negative Binomial regression
- XGBoost
