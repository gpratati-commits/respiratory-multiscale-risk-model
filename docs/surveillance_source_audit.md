# Singapore Respiratory Surveillance Source Audit

## Objective

Determine which respiratory surveillance variables are sufficiently
consistent for inclusion in the forecasting study.

## Primary Outcome

Candidate:

Average daily polyclinic attendance for Upper Respiratory Tract
Infection (URTI).

## Candidate Pathogen Predictors

### Influenza

Questions:

- Is a weekly measure available?
- What is the measure?
- What years are available?
- Is the definition consistent?
- Are there missing periods?

Potential measures:

- Influenza positivity
- Influenza-like illness activity
- Laboratory-confirmed influenza activity

Status: TO BE ASSESSED


### RSV

Questions:

- Is a weekly surveillance series available?
- What years are available?
- Is reporting consistent?
- Are there large missing periods?
- Is the measure laboratory positivity, cases, or another indicator?

Status: TO BE ASSESSED


### SARS-CoV-2

Questions:

- Which weekly measure is most consistent?
- When does the series begin?
- Are cases, hospitalisations, or positivity most suitable?
- Did surveillance definitions change substantially over time?

Status: TO BE ASSESSED


## Inclusion Rule

A predictor will be included in the main forecasting model only if:

1. Its epidemiological meaning is clear.
2. Its temporal resolution is suitable.
3. It overlaps sufficiently with the URTI outcome.
4. Reporting definitions are reasonably consistent.
5. Missingness does not make the series unreliable.

Shorter series may be used in sensitivity analyses rather than
forcing them into the primary model.


## Study Period

The study period will be selected AFTER surveillance availability
and consistency have been assessed.

The study period will not be chosen simply to maximize sample size.
## Preliminary Findings

### URTI / ARI

The sampled 2019, 2023 and 2026 bulletins all report population-level
respiratory burden using average daily polyclinic attendance for acute
upper respiratory infection / acute respiratory infection.

This outcome appears broadly comparable across the sampled years,
although terminology and any reporting-definition changes will continue
to be assessed.

### Influenza

The influenza surveillance measure is not identical across the sampled years.

In 2019 and 2023, the bulletin reports:

- proportion of ILI among ARI polyclinic attendances
- influenza positivity among ILI samples over the previous 4 weeks

In 2026, the bulletin reports:

- weekly influenza positivity among ILI samples for the current epidemiological week

Therefore, influenza positivity should not yet be treated as one
continuous historical variable.

The transition year and exact definition change must be identified before
constructing the forecasting dataset.

### RSV

No named RSV surveillance measure was identified in the sampled 2019,
2023 or 2026 Week 40 bulletins.

RSV will not be forced into the main forecasting model unless a separate
consistent surveillance source is identified.

### SARS-CoV-2

COVID-19 positivity among ARI samples is explicitly reported in the
2026 bulletin but not in the sampled 2019 or 2023 bulletins.

SARS-CoV-2 is therefore likely to be suitable only for a more recent
secondary analysis unless a separate consistent historical source is found.
